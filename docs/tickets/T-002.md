# T-002: Exact minimum for up to 12 members

Status: done
Milestone: M1
Risk: high
Issue: #5

## Goal

For a group of up to 12 members with a non-zero balance, settle up returns the true minimum number of payments; above that it keeps the greedy method and says so.

## Done when

1. On random ledgers with at most 7 members with a non-zero balance (seeded `random.Random`, at least 300 cases), the number of payments equals the minimum found by a brute-force oracle over all set partitions (n minus the largest number of zero-sum groups). Checked by a test in `tests/test_settle.py`.
2. For balances {A: +5, B: +4, C: −4, D: −3, E: −2} (as hundreds of cents), the exact method gives 3 payments where greedy gives 4. Checked by `tests/test_settle.py::test_exact_beats_greedy_when_a_smaller_group_clears_itself`. (Corrected after the build, D-6: the original example {+5, −5, +3, −3} gives 2 payments with greedy in every order, so it could not show the difference.)
3. With 12 members with a non-zero balance, `settle_up` returns in under 1 second. Checked by a timed test in `tests/test_settle.py`.
4. With 13 or more such members it uses the greedy method and `tally settle-up` prints the "may not be minimal" note on stderr; with 12 or fewer the note is not printed. Checked by tests in `tests/test_settle.py` and `tests/test_cli.py`.
5. T-001's conditions 1 and 2 still hold for both methods, and the full test command passes: `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q`.

## Assumptions

- Exact method: dynamic programming over subsets in O(n·2^n): `dp[mask] = max over i in mask of dp[mask − i] + (1 if sum(mask) == 0)`. Recover the zero-sum groups from the table, then settle each group with the greedy method (a group of m gives m−1 payments).
- ADR 0001 states O(3^n); O(n·2^n) is cheaper and keeps the decision (cap of 12) unchanged. Raising the cap would need a new ADR superseding 0001.
- `cli.py` is in scope only to show or hide the stderr note.
